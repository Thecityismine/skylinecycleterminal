Bitcoin and Ethereum move together — until they don't. Anyone who's watched a full cycle has seen it: for long stretches ETH simply tracks BTC, and then, at certain points, it breaks away and moves with a character all its own. Understanding **how the two cycles relate** is one of the most practical skills in crypto, because it shapes when Ethereum tends to outperform, when it lags, and what that rotation says about where the broader cycle stands.

This guide covers how BTC and ETH cycles rhyme, where they diverge, and how to read the all-important ETH/BTC ratio.

> Educational only — not financial advice. This is cycle analysis, not a recommendation or a prediction about either asset.

---

## They rhyme, but they don't copy

Bitcoin is the gravitational center of crypto. It's the largest asset, the primary liquidity magnet, and the benchmark everything else is measured against. Ethereum, as the largest "risk-on" alternative, sits one notch further out on the risk curve.

That relationship produces a consistent pattern:

- **Both follow the same broad cycle** — spring, summer, autumn, winter happen to both. When Bitcoin is in a bull season, Ethereum usually is too; when Bitcoin enters winter, Ethereum rarely escapes it.
- **Ethereum moves with higher beta.** Being further out on the risk curve, ETH tends to fall harder in downturns and rally harder in the strong part of an uptrend. Same cycle, bigger swings.
- **Their timing diverges.** Bitcoin often *leads* — especially early in a cycle, when fresh capital tends to enter through BTC first. Ethereum and the broader alt market often *follow*, outperforming later in the uptrend once risk appetite is high.

So ETH's cycle is best understood as **Bitcoin's cycle, amplified and slightly delayed** — not a separate rhythm.

## The signal that ties them together: the ETH/BTC ratio

The **ETH/BTC ratio** — Ethereum's price measured in Bitcoin rather than dollars — is one of the most useful indicators for understanding capital rotation between Bitcoin and Ethereum. It strips out the overall market direction and answers one question: *is Ethereum outperforming or underperforming Bitcoin right now?*

- **ETH/BTC rising** → Ethereum is outperforming. Historically this has tended to happen when risk appetite is high — often later in an uptrend, and frequently alongside broader "altseason" behavior.
- **ETH/BTC falling** → Bitcoin is outperforming. This is common early in a cycle recovery and during risk-off periods, when capital concentrates in Bitcoin as the safer crypto bet.

Read alongside Skyline's other market-structure signals, the ratio tells you three things at a glance: **when ETH begins to outperform BTC, when capital rotates back into Bitcoin, and whether the market is entering an ETH leadership phase.** It's a core chart precisely because it captures rotation *within* crypto — a different and complementary read to whether crypto as a whole is going up or down.

---

## How capital tends to rotate

A recurring (not guaranteed) rotation has played out across past cycles:

1. **Bitcoin leads first.** Early in a recovery, new money favors BTC. Bitcoin dominance rises; ETH/BTC often falls even as both gain in dollar terms.
2. **Ethereum catches up.** As the uptrend matures and confidence grows, capital rotates out along the risk curve into ETH. ETH/BTC turns up.
3. **Alts follow ETH.** In the most risk-hungry phase, capital rotates further still into the broader altcoin market — the classic "altseason."
4. **Risk-off reverses it.** When the cycle turns, the flow runs backward: alts bleed first, then ETH, and capital retreats toward Bitcoin.

Knowing roughly where you are in that rotation is a strong clue to how mature the cycle's risk appetite has become — and late-stage risk appetite is itself a cycle signal.

---

## What this means in practice

- **Read ETH through BTC's cycle first.** Ethereum rarely fights the broader Bitcoin season; identify the season, then ask how ETH is positioned within it.
- **Use ETH/BTC for rotation, dollar price for direction.** They answer different questions — keep them separate.
- **Treat late-cycle ETH/alt outperformance as information.** A euphoric rotation into ETH and alts often coincides with the later, higher-risk part of a cycle — worth pairing with the top signals in [How to Identify Bitcoin Tops](/learn/how-to-identify-bitcoin-tops).
- **Remember the beta cuts both ways.** ETH's amplification is as real on the downside as the upside.

## The takeaways

- BTC and ETH **share one cycle**; ETH moves with **higher beta** and often **lags** BTC's timing.
- The **ETH/BTC ratio** shows which is outperforming — rising = ETH leads (risk-on), falling = BTC leads (risk-off / early cycle).
- Capital tends to **rotate** BTC → ETH → alts → back to BTC across a cycle.
- Use **ETH/BTC for rotation, dollar price for direction**, and read ETH through Bitcoin's season.
