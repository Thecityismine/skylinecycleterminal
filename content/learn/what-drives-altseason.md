"Altseason" is one of the most hyped and least understood ideas in crypto. To most people it's a vibe, that stretch when altcoins suddenly fly and everyone feels like a genius. But altseason isn't random luck. It's the product of a specific, repeatable **rotation of capital** that tends to happen at a particular point in the cycle. Understand the mechanics, and altseason stops being a mystery and becomes a *condition you can recognize.*

This guide explains what altseason actually is, the forces that drive it, and, just as important, why it's usually a **late-cycle** signal worth reading with care.

> Educational only, not financial advice. This explains a market pattern; it is not a recommendation about any asset, and altcoins carry higher risk than Bitcoin.

---

## What altseason actually is

Altseason is a period when **altcoins broadly outperform Bitcoin**, when capital rotates out of BTC and into the rest of the market, and alt prices rise faster than Bitcoin (in both dollar and BTC terms).

The key word is *rotation.* Altseason isn't usually new money arriving from outside crypto. It's existing crypto capital moving **further out on the risk curve**, from Bitcoin, to Ethereum, to large-cap alts, and eventually to the smallest and most speculative coins.

---

## The capital rotation that creates it

Altseason is the third act of a predictable (not guaranteed) rotation across the cycle:

1. **Bitcoin leads.** Early in a cycle, fresh capital enters through Bitcoin, the safest, most liquid crypto asset. Bitcoin dominance rises.
2. **Ethereum catches up.** As confidence grows, capital rotates into ETH, the largest risk-on alternative. The ETH/BTC ratio turns up. *(See [Bitcoin vs Ethereum Cycles](/learn/bitcoin-vs-ethereum-cycles).)*
3. **Alts follow, altseason.** In the most risk-hungry phase, capital rotates further still into the broader altcoin market. This is altseason: profits from BTC and ETH chasing higher returns down the risk curve.
4. **Risk-off reverses it.** When the cycle turns, the flow runs backward, alts bleed first and hardest, and capital retreats toward Bitcoin.

Altseason is act three. That's why it tends to appear **late** in a bull cycle, not early.

---

## The forces behind it

Several conditions typically line up for altseason to run:

- **High risk appetite.** Altcoins are the riskiest end of crypto. They rally hardest when the crowd is most confident, often into euphoria.
- **Ample liquidity and dry powder.** Rotation needs fuel. Low [stablecoin dominance](/learn/stablecoin-dominance-explained) later in a cycle shows that dry powder is being deployed down the risk curve.
- **Falling Bitcoin dominance.** When BTC dominance rolls over while the total market holds up, it signals capital leaving Bitcoin *for alts* rather than exiting crypto entirely. This is one of the clearest altseason tells.
- **A maturing uptrend.** Altseason feeds on the confidence built during Bitcoin's and Ethereum's earlier gains, it needs a trend already in motion.

---

## Why it's a caution signal, not just an opportunity

Here's the part the hype skips: a full-blown altseason, especially in the most speculative coins, has historically coincided with the **later, higher-risk stages of a cycle**, often near the top.

The same euphoria that powers altseason is the emotional signature of a late cycle. When the riskiest assets are going vertical and everyone feels invincible, the market is usually closer to autumn than spring. That doesn't mean altseason can't run further, it often does, and violently, but it should be read alongside the [top signals](/learn/how-to-identify-bitcoin-tops), not in isolation from them. Altseason is as much a *risk gauge* as an opportunity.

---

## How to read the conditions: the three dominance charts

The rotation story is told most clearly by three dominance readings, watched together:

- **Bitcoin Dominance**, BTC's share of total crypto market cap. When it **rolls over** while the market holds up, capital is leaving Bitcoin *for* other assets. The primary altseason tell.
- **Ethereum Dominance**, ETH's share. A **rising** ETH dominance signals Ethereum leadership, usually the first step out along the risk curve, and often the gateway to a broader altseason.
- **Stablecoin Dominance**, the sidelined cash. **Falling** stablecoin dominance shows that dry powder is being deployed into risk rather than held. *(See [Stablecoin Dominance & Liquidity](/learn/stablecoin-dominance-explained).)*

Together, falling BTC dominance, rising ETH dominance, falling stablecoin dominance, they trace the classic rotation footprint. A few more principles:

- **Watch ETH/BTC alongside them.** A rising ETH/BTC ratio confirms the rotation into Ethereum.
- **Anchor it to cycle position.** Altseason arriving while the broader cycle is already stretched is a very different message from early-cycle strength.
- **Respect the beta.** Alts amplify moves in *both* directions: the same force that makes altseason exhilarating makes the reversal brutal.

## The takeaways

- **Altseason** = altcoins broadly outperforming Bitcoin as capital rotates down the risk curve.
- It's driven by **high risk appetite, ample dry powder, falling BTC dominance, and a maturing uptrend.**
- It's usually a **late-cycle** event, an opportunity *and* a caution signal.
- Read it via **BTC dominance, ETH/BTC, and stablecoin dominance**, anchored to cycle position.
